Rules for SBCC

The Single Board Computer Competition (SBCC) has set the following rules to ensure fair and equal competition among participants.

All Apple chips are off limits.

Participants must use a minimum of 6 sockets of the same type (any additional sockets may be of any type) and run MPI. The power limit is set at 250 watts for the compute cluster, and an additional 50 watts for networking/interconnect equipment.

The cost limit is $6,000 US Dollars. MSRP or used pricing is acceptable. Teams using used pricing must provide proof of at least 3 comparable used listings at or near the declared price.

Laptops are banned by default. A laptop or laptop-derived system whose hardware configuration is substantially similar to an existing SBC may be considered for approval at the committee's sole discretion. Similarity to an SBC does not guarantee approval. All such systems are reviewed on a case-by-case basis.

Teams are required to use a smart wall plug or equivalent wall-power monitoring device as proof of power consumption. Setting up a web-accessible monitoring interface such as Grafana is encouraged. If a separate purchase of a smart wall plug is required, please contact the committee.

The competition will include benchmarking and application challenges.

The SBCC competition will be held virtually. The event will take place during April 2027. Participants are only allowed to set up and touch the physical cluster hardware on the first day from 8:00 am to 5:00 pm local time. Participants may only be connected to the cluster during official competition hours from 8:00 am to 5:00 pm local time each day. Clusters may continue to run jobs outside of official competition hours if a job scheduler was started before 5:00 pm local time each day.

To measure efficiency, the GFLOPS/watt for the chips being tested will be recorded based on HPL results. A sample result of what is expected can be found here: https://github.com/deater/performance_results. We just want a table with the chips and the gflops/watt.

The SBCC competition presents a unique opportunity for participants to showcase their skills in building and optimizing single board computer clusters, while also gaining valuable knowledge and experience in the field of high-performance computing.
